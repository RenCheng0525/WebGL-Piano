# WebGL Piano

Live Demo: [https://rencheng0525.github.io/WebGL-Piano/](https://rencheng0525.github.io/WebGL-Piano/)

Generated by Claude with prompts only, no manual code editing.

## Overview

A browser-based 3D piano that plays MIDI files with synchronized falling notes and automatically generated sheet music. It uses Three.js for rendering, Tone.js for audio, @tonejs/midi for parsing, and abcjs for music engraving. You can also play it yourself with a mouse, touch screen, computer keyboard, or a real MIDI keyboard. The interface is available in Traditional Chinese and English.

The whole app is a single self-contained HTML file. The piano samples are embedded in the file, so it works without a separate audio server.

## Features

### MIDI playback

- **Reliable MIDI parsing**: Uses @tonejs/midi, which handles tempo changes, running status, SysEx, long meta events, and Format 0 and Format 1 files.
- **Sustain pedal support**: CC64 is applied, so notes ring until the pedal is released. Re-striking the same note cuts the previous one, as on a real piano.
- **Accurate timing**: Notes are scheduled with `Tone.Transport` and `Tone.Part` instead of `requestAnimationFrame`. Playback stays in time even when the tab is in the background.
- **Drum tracks skipped**: Channel 10 percussion is ignored rather than played on the piano.
- **Track names decoded**: Non-UTF-8 track names (Big5, GBK, Shift_JIS) are decoded correctly.

### Sheet music

When a MIDI file is loaded, it is transcribed into a grand staff and shown in a panel above the piano. If transcription fails, the panel closes and a message explains why.

- **Rhythm quantization**: Each beat is snapped to the simplest grid that fits: 16th notes, eighth-note triplets, or 32nd notes. Small timing deviations from live playing are absorbed.
- **Meter changes and pickups**: All time signatures in the file are used. A short first measure followed by the main meter is treated as a pickup bar, and measure numbering starts after it.
- **Key detection**: The file’s key signature is used when it fits the music. Otherwise the key is detected with the Krumhansl–Schmuckler algorithm.
- **Correct spelling**: Accidentals are spelled according to the key (for example, the leading tone in a minor key is always written as a sharp), and courtesy naturals follow standard rules within a measure.
- **Hands and voices**: Notes are split between the treble and bass staves by track or by pitch. Each staff can hold two voices, so held notes and moving lines are written separately.
- **Clean notation**: Short articulation or pedal gaps are absorbed instead of becoming rests, ties cross beats and barlines correctly, beams follow the meter, and fast runs are kept in order instead of collapsing into chords.
- **Synchronized playback**: A cursor follows the music, the current notes light up, and the score scrolls automatically (this can be turned off). Click any note to jump to that point.
- **Zoom and layout**: The score can be zoomed, and line breaks adapt to the panel width.

### Visuals

- **Falling notes**: A Synthesia-style waterfall shows upcoming notes, colored by track. It is drawn with a single `InstancedMesh` for performance.
- **Full 88-key keyboard**: A0 to C8 with correct white and black key layout.
- **Key animation**: Keys sink according to velocity and light up in their track’s color.
- **Resizable layout**: Drag the divider between the score and the piano to change their sizes. The piano camera reframes automatically, and the size is remembered in the browser.
- **Light and dark themes**: The interface, score, and scene follow the system color scheme.

### Languages

- **Traditional Chinese and English**: Every part of the interface is translated, including buttons, messages, the score description (for example, “E♭ 大調，12/8 拍” or “E♭ major, 12/8 time”), the demo, and screen-reader labels. English text handles singular and plural forms.
- **Automatic choice**: On the first visit, the language follows the browser. Chinese browsers get Traditional Chinese, and all others get English.
- **Instant switching**: The language button in the playback bar switches languages without reloading. The choice is remembered in the browser.
- Track names and titles stored in MIDI files are shown as written, and General MIDI instrument names stay in English.

### Audio

- **30 Salamander Grand Piano samples**: One sample every minor third, for much less pitch-shifting distortion than a 4-sample set.
- **Velocity-sensitive dynamics**: MIDI velocity controls loudness.
- **Reverb and limiter**: Light room reverb, with a limiter to prevent clipping in dense passages.

### Controls

- Play, pause, and stop.
- A progress bar you can click or drag to seek. Arrow keys skip 5 seconds when it is focused.
- Playback speed from 0.5× to 2×.
- Volume control.
- A language button for switching between Traditional Chinese and English.
- A track panel for muting individual tracks.
- Drag-and-drop file loading.
- A built-in demo: the first 19 bars of Bach’s Prelude in C major, BWV 846.
- A hint bar with keyboard shortcuts, MIDI connection, and camera reset. It appears when the pointer is over the playback bar, or for a few seconds after tapping it on touch devices.

### Playing live

| Input | How |
| --- | --- |
| Mouse or touch | Click or tap keys. Drag across keys for a glissando. Multi-touch is supported. |
| Computer keyboard | `A W S E D F T G Y H U J K` play one octave starting at C. `O L P ;` continue upward. `Z` and `X` shift the octave. `Space` plays or pauses. |
| MIDI keyboard | Click “連接 MIDI 鍵盤” to connect through Web MIDI. The sustain pedal works too. |
| Camera | Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or two-finger drag to pan. “重設視角” resets the view. |
| Score panel | The staff button in the playback bar shows or hides the score. Drag the divider to resize it. Double-click the divider, or press `Home` while it is focused, to restore the default size. `↑` and `↓` resize it from the keyboard. |

## Transcription limits

Turning MIDI back into notation means guessing information that MIDI does not store, so some results differ from a published score.

- Ornaments, grace notes, and free cadenzas are written out as exact rhythms, usually as runs of 32nd notes.
- Irregular tuplets such as quintuplets are snapped to the nearest supported grid.
- Recordings with heavy rubato quantize less cleanly than files exported from notation software.
- Some files use extra time signatures to fit cadenzas, so the measure count can differ from the printed edition.

## Tech stack

- [Three.js](https://threejs.org/) r128 with OrbitControls
- [Tone.js](https://tonejs.github.io/) 14.8.49
- [@tonejs/midi](https://github.com/Tonejs/Midi) 2.0.28
- [abcjs](https://www.abcjs.net/) 6.7.1

## Browser support

- Recent versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari with WebGL.
- Web MIDI input works in Chrome and Edge. Firefox and Safari do not support it.
- On phones, the camera frames the middle of the keyboard. Pan with two fingers to reach the rest.
