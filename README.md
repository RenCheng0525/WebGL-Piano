# WebGL Piano

Live Demo: [https://rencheng0525.github.io/WebGL-Piano/](https://rencheng0525.github.io/WebGL-Piano/)

Generated by Claude with prompts only, no manual code editing.

## Overview

A browser-based 3D piano that plays MIDI and MusicXML files with synchronized falling notes and sheet music. Open a file and the piano plays it, notes fall toward the keys, and a cursor follows along in the score. You can also play it yourself with a mouse, touch screen, computer keyboard, or a real MIDI keyboard. The interface is available in Traditional Chinese and English.

The whole app is a single self-contained HTML file. The piano samples are embedded in the file, so it works without a separate audio server.

## Two kinds of input: MIDI and MusicXML

### What is MusicXML?

MIDI and MusicXML both describe music, but they describe different things.

- **MIDI** is a performance format. It records which key was pressed, when, how hard, and for how long. It is ideal for playback, but it contains no notation: no clefs, key signatures, beaming, octave lines, dynamics markings, or expression text.
- **MusicXML** is a notation format. It is an open, XML-based standard for exchanging sheet music between programs such as MuseScore, Finale, Sibelius, and Dorico. It stores the score itself: notes and rhythms as written, plus clefs, key and time signatures, ottava lines, articulations, dynamics, fingerings, lyrics, titles, and composers. Files come either as plain XML (`.musicxml`, `.xml`) or compressed (`.mxl`).

Because of this, the app treats the two formats differently:

| | MIDI (`.mid`) | MusicXML (`.musicxml`, `.xml`, `.mxl`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sheet music | Transcribed automatically from the performance data | The original score, exactly as edited |
| Engraving | abcjs | OpenSheetMusicDisplay |
| Score detail | Pitches, rhythms, key, meter, ottava lines, clef changes | Everything in the file, including dynamics, articulations, ornaments, and text |
| 3D piano and falling notes | Yes | Yes (notes are read from the MusicXML) |
| Score cursor and auto-scroll | Yes | Yes |
| Click a note to jump there | Yes | Not yet; use the progress bar |

If you want a clean score, MusicXML is the better choice. Many scores on sites such as MuseScore can be downloaded as MusicXML.

## Features

### Playback

- **Accurate timing**: Notes are scheduled with `Tone.Transport` and `Tone.Part` instead of `requestAnimationFrame`. Playback stays in time even when the tab is in the background.
- **Sustain pedal**: MIDI CC64 and MusicXML pedal markings are applied, so notes ring until the pedal is released. Re-striking the same note cuts the previous one, as on a real piano.
- **Dynamics**: MIDI velocity, and MusicXML dynamics from *pppp* to *ffff*, control loudness.
- **Tempo changes**: Tempo maps in MIDI files and tempo markings in MusicXML are followed.
- **Drum tracks skipped**: MIDI percussion tracks are ignored rather than played on the piano.
- **Track names decoded**: Non-UTF-8 MIDI track names (Big5, GBK, Shift_JIS) are decoded correctly.

### MusicXML

- **Original engraving**: Scores are rendered with OpenSheetMusicDisplay, so they look like the published edition.
- **Playback from the score**: Pitches, rhythms, chords, multiple voices, ties, tempo markings, dynamics, and pedal markings are read from the file. Each staff of a piano part becomes its own colored track.
- **Compressed files**: `.mxl` files are unzipped in the browser with the built-in `DecompressionStream`, with no extra library.
- **Synchronized cursor**: A cursor follows playback and the score scrolls automatically (this can be turned off).

### Automatic transcription of MIDI

When a MIDI file is loaded, it is transcribed into a grand staff.

- **Rhythm quantization**: Each beat is snapped to the simplest grid that fits: 16th notes, eighth-note triplets, or 32nd notes. Small timing deviations from live playing are absorbed.
- **Meter changes and pickups**: All time signatures in the file are used. A short first measure followed by the main meter is treated as a pickup bar, and measure numbering starts after it.
- **Key**: The file’s key signature is used when it has sharps or flats, choosing between the major key and its relative minor. Otherwise the key is detected with the Krumhansl–Schmuckler algorithm, with a bonus for keys whose tonic chord ends the piece.
- **Correct spelling**: Accidentals follow the key (for example, the leading tone in a minor key is written as a sharp or double sharp), and courtesy naturals follow standard rules within a measure.
- **Readable layout**: Very high passages are written with 8va lines instead of many ledger lines, and each staff switches between treble and bass clef as the range requires.
- **Hands and voices**: Notes are split between the staves by track or by pitch. Each staff can hold two voices, so held notes and moving lines are written separately.
- **Clean notation**: Short articulation or pedal gaps are absorbed instead of becoming rests, ties cross beats and barlines correctly, beams follow the meter, and fast runs stay in order instead of collapsing into chords.
- **Synchronized playback**: The current notes light up, and clicking any note jumps to that point.

### Visuals and layout

- **Full 88-key keyboard**: A0 to C8. Keys sink according to velocity and light up in their track’s color.
- **Falling notes**: A Synthesia-style waterfall shows upcoming notes, drawn with a single `InstancedMesh` for performance.
- **Resizable layout**: Drag the divider between the score and the piano to change their sizes. The camera reframes automatically, and the size is remembered in the browser.
- **Light and dark themes**: The interface, score, and scene follow the system color scheme.

### Audio

- **30 Salamander Grand Piano samples**: One sample every minor third, for much less pitch-shifting distortion than a 4-sample set.
- **Reverb and limiter**: Light room reverb, with a limiter to prevent clipping in dense passages.

### Languages

- **Traditional Chinese and English**: Every part of the interface is translated, including messages, the score description, the demo, and screen-reader labels.
- **Automatic choice**: The language follows the browser on the first visit. The language button switches instantly, and the choice is remembered.
- Track names and titles stored in files are shown as written.

## Controls

- Play, pause, and stop.
- A progress bar you can click or drag to seek. Arrow keys skip 5 seconds when it is focused.
- Playback speed from 0.5× to 2×, and volume.
- A track panel for muting individual tracks.
- Opening files with the button, or by drag-and-drop.
- A built-in demo: the first 19 bars of Bach’s Prelude in C major, BWV 846.
- A hint bar with keyboard shortcuts, MIDI connection, and camera reset. It appears when the pointer is over the playback bar, or for a few seconds after tapping it on touch devices.

| Input | How |
| --- | --- |
| Mouse or touch | Click or tap keys. Drag across keys for a glissando. Multi-touch is supported. |
| Computer keyboard | `A W S E D F T G Y H U J K` play one octave starting at C. `O L P ;` continue upward. `Z` and `X` shift the octave. `Space` plays or pauses. |
| MIDI keyboard | Connect through Web MIDI from the hint bar. The sustain pedal works too. |
| Camera | Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or two-finger drag to pan. “Reset view” restores the default. |
| Score panel | The staff button shows or hides the score. Drag the divider to resize it. Double-click the divider, or press `Home` while it is focused, to restore the default size. `↑` and `↓` resize it from the keyboard. |

## Limitations

### MIDI transcription

Turning MIDI back into notation means guessing information that MIDI does not store, so results differ from a published score.

- Dynamics, articulations, fermatas, and expression text are not in MIDI and cannot be recovered.
- Ornaments, grace notes, and free cadenzas are written out as exact rhythms, usually as runs of 32nd notes.
- Irregular tuplets such as quintuplets are snapped to the nearest supported grid.
- Recordings with heavy rubato quantize less cleanly than files exported from notation software.
- Some files use extra time signatures to fit cadenzas, so the measure count can differ from the printed edition.

### MusicXML

- Repeats, D.C., and D.S. are not expanded. The score is played once in written order.
- Fermatas and tempo-free markings such as *ritardando* do not change playback timing, so total length can be a little shorter than in MuseScore.
- Only `score-partwise` files are supported (the format used by almost all programs).
- The engraving engine (about 1 MB) is downloaded the first time a MusicXML file is opened, so it needs a network connection.

## Tech stack

- [Three.js](https://threejs.org/) r128 with OrbitControls
- [Tone.js](https://tonejs.github.io/) 14.8.49
- [@tonejs/midi](https://github.com/Tonejs/Midi) 2.0.28, for reading MIDI files
- [abcjs](https://www.abcjs.net/) 6.7.1, for engraving transcribed MIDI
- [OpenSheetMusicDisplay](https://opensheetmusicdisplay.org/) 2.2.0, for rendering MusicXML

## Browser support

- Recent versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari with WebGL. Opening `.mxl` files requires `DecompressionStream` (Chrome 80+, Firefox 113+, Safari 16.4+).
- Web MIDI input works in Chrome and Edge. Firefox and Safari do not support it.
- On phones, the camera frames the middle of the keyboard. Pan with two fingers to reach the rest.
